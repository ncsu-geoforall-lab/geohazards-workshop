Workshop data

Large rasters are not committed. scripts/fetch_data.sh downloads the prepared GRASS project from the repository’s GitHub Release. This folder holds the area of interest, the source lists, and the provenance record.

Study area

Humbug Creek, HUC12 180102060801, Siskiyou County, California (95.7 km2). The creek burned at high severity in the 2022 McKinney Fire (ignition 2022-07-29). On 2022-08-02 a monsoonal storm cell dropped roughly 4 in of rain on the burn scar. A fire control line follows the creek, so unburned terrain sits next to burned channels.

  • aoi/HumbugCreek.geojson: HUC12 boundary from the Watershed Boundary Dataset, EPSG:4269.
  • aoi/focal.geojson: the workshop region, the bbox in Tim Bailey’s OpenTopography link (-122.7530, 41.7727, -122.7173, 41.7922). About 3.0 x 2.2 km at 1 m, all inside B22 tile x52y463. The full HUC12 is the optional extension.
  • Fire perimeter: MTBS McKinney Fire, CA4183012289520220729 (Extended assessment, post-fire scene 2023-08-15), fetched from the USFS EDW MTBS map service by scripts/prepare_data.py.

Elevation epochs

Collection dates come from the 3DEP WESM index and the OpenTopography catalog, checked 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-15.

Epoch Dataset Collected Horizontal CRS Vertical datum Covers focal area
2010 CA_OR_KLAMATHRIVER_2010 (legacy) 2010-02-27 to 2010-03-15 EPSG:2870, NAD83(HARN) CA zone 1, ftUS NAVD88 ftUS, GEOID09 no
2013 USFS Humbug Creek Lidar, CA 2013 (OpenTopography CA13_Humbug) 2013-09-04 to 2013-09-07 EPSG:26910, NAD83 UTM 10N NAVD88, GEOID12A yes
2018 CA_Klamath_Topography_2018 2018-06-01 to 2018-06-14 EPSG:6339, NAD83(2011) UTM 10N NAVD88, GEOID12B no, north strip only
2022 CA_NorthernCA_6_B22 2022-09-14 to 2022-10-28 EPSG:6339, NAD83(2011) UTM 10N NAVD88, GEOID18 yes

The 2022 collection came 6 to 12 weeks after the storm and before the winter rains, so it records the immediate rain-on-fire response.

The epochs use different NAD83 realizations and different geoid models. That difference is part of the lesson on co-registration. Do not hide it in preprocessing.

  • Both the 2013 file and the USGS 1 m tiles declare EPSG:26910 in their headers, so the realization is only in the collection metadata. The GRASS project uses EPSG:26910 and nothing is reprojected on import.
  • Treating the 2013 data as NAD83(1986) and transforming to NAD83(2011) moves a point at the site by 0.20 m east and 0.55 m north with the NADCON5 grids, and by 0 m when PROJ cannot reach them. Reprojecting would make the result depend on network access.
  • GEOID18 minus GEOID12B undulation across the focal region is -0.021 to -0.027 m, so 2022 heights read 2 to 3 cm higher for the same ellipsoid height. GEOID12B stands in for GEOID12A here. NGS documents GEOID12B as identical to GEOID12A except in Puerto Rico (GEOID12B technical details).

Getting the 2013 DTM

OpenTopography distributes the 2013 USFS DTM (raster ID OTSDEM.122022.26910.2, https://doi.org/10.5069/G9FJ2F12) only after you sign in, and it is not in the public bulk buckets. Download it once for the focal region and pass it to scripts/prepare_data.py --dtm2013. Participants get the clipped copy from the release.

The download box in EPSG:26910 is the focal region plus the 100 m clip buffer: xmin 520423, ymin 4624467, xmax 523597, ymax 4626838. Choose GeoTIFF output with the bare-earth and highest-hit layers, then pass output_be.tif as --dtm2013 and output_hh.tif as --dsm2013. Highest hit minus bare earth gives pre-fire canopy height for part 02. Cite the dataset as listed under References.

The delivered 2013 grid starts at 520422.29, 4626838.83, so its cells sit 0.29 m east and 0.17 m south of the whole-meter grid the 2022 data and the focal region use. r.import keeps that grid because the CRS already matches. Notebook 01 resamples the 2013 DTM onto the region with r.resamp.interp (bilinear). Reading the native grid on the region instead takes the nearest cell, which shifts the surface by that offset and changes the DoD by up to 2 m on steep slopes.

Noise baseline

  • The 2010 legacy collection does not cover the focal region, so the noise baseline comes from stable unburned ground across the control line. About 15 percent of the focal region is unburned.

Burn severity

dnbr and burn_severity in PERMANENT (10 m) come from two Sentinel-2 L2A scenes on Element84 Earth Search, imported with t.stac.item by scripts/prepare_data.py:

  • Pre-fire: S2B_10TEM_20220721_0_L2A, eight days before ignition.
  • Post-fire: S2B_10TEM_20220909_0_L2A, after the 2022-08-02 storm and five days before the 2022 lidar collection began.

B12 is natively 20 m, so NBR has an effective resolution of 20 m resampled to 10 m. NBR is (B08 - B12) / (B08 + B12) on reflectance, and dnbr is 1000 x (NBR pre - NBR post) minus its median outside the fire perimeter (2.4).

Reflectance is DN x 1e-4. The Earth Search items list a raster:bands offset of -0.1 and set earthsearch:boa_offset_applied: true, and the pixels already have the processing baseline 04.00 offset removed. Applying -0.1 again pushes 20% of pre-fire cells to near-zero reflectance and moves the unburned median dNBR to -25; without it the unburned median is +2.

The classes use the dNBR thresholds MTBS published for this fire (CA4183012289520220729): increased greenness below -150, unburned to low below 35, low below 300, moderate below 530, high from 530. MTBS derived them from a Landsat extended assessment (post-fire scene 2023-08-15), so they approximate classes for this Sentinel-2 initial assessment.

The build falls back to a local perimeter (--perimeter) when the USFS MTBS service is unavailable.

True color

s2_pre_red, s2_pre_green, s2_pre_blue and the matching s2_post_* rasters in PERMANENT are the 10 m red, green, and blue bands (B04, B03, B02) of the same two Sentinel-2 scenes, as reflectance x 10000. Every band of both dates has the same linear color table, black at 0 and white at 0.2 reflectance, so d.rgb shows both dates on one scale. The 98th percentile of the post-fire red band is 0.2.

Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2022.

Streams

nhd_flowlines in PERMANENT holds the USGS National Hydrography Dataset large-scale flowlines over the HUC12 bounding box, with gnis_name, ftype, fcode, and lengthkm. scripts/prepare_data.py fetches them from the NHD map service (layer 6) and keeps the GeoJSON in raw/nhd_flowlines.geojson. The build falls back to a local copy (--flowlines) when the service is unavailable. The notebooks still derive channels from the 2022 DTM for the analysis. The NHD lines are for orientation.
